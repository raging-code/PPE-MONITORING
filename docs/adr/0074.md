# ADR 0074: edit a firefighter profile and withdraw consent (S8-5a host, S8-5b dashboard)

Status: Proposed (decided with the operator on 2026-11-28; confirm at the next design review, and the data-governance owner confirms decision C).

Design: Rev 3.45 stays frozen. This ADR adds two endpoints to the REST list of design 3.8 (20 to 22), one column and two audit actions (Migration 0008, audit_actions 28 to 30). No wire message, no setting and no new error code.

## Why

ADR 0070 to 0073 can add a person with consent, assign them, set them inactive and export their readings. Two things were missing. Weight, age and heart rate change over time, and a wrong value could only be fixed by editing the database directly, which leaves no audit row. And there was no way to honour a withdrawal of consent under RA 10173: a person who stopped agreeing could only be set inactive, and their readings stayed in the per-firefighter export.

## Decisions

- **A. Edit a profile.** `POST /api/firefighters/edit` {firefighter_id, and any of full_name, sex, age_years, height_cm, weight_kg, resting_hr, max_hr, smoker, years_service} -> {firefighter_id, alias, result, changed}. Only the fields sent change. sex, max_hr and years_service sent as null are cleared; the other fields cannot be null. The ranges are those of a new profile (services/firefighters.RANGES) and max_hr must stay above resting_hr after the change, whichever of the two moves. A request whose values are all what they are is `unchanged` and writes nothing. The alias and the consent record (date, form version, witness) are not editable: a wrong consent record is a matter for the governance owner, not a form field.
- **B. The audit row names fields, never values.** `FIREFIGHTER_EDITED` with the operator and the reason `firefighter_id 1 (FFR-01) changed: age_years, weight_kg`. No value and no name. The old value is not kept anywhere, so after an edit the log says that a field changed and when, not from what. (Open point below.)
- **C. Withdraw consent.** `POST /api/firefighters/withdraw` {firefighter_id} -> {firefighter_id, alias, result} with result `withdrawn` or `unchanged`. It stores the time in the new column `firefighters.consent_withdrawn_ts`, sets the person inactive and ends the jacket they wear, in one transaction. Nothing is deleted: the profile and the consent record stay as the record of what was agreed and when it was withdrawn. One audit row, `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN`, with the operator and the reason `firefighter_id 1 (FFR-01) consent withdrawn`. A repeat writes no row. The date stored is the time the operator pressed the button, not the date on a paper form.
- **D. It cannot be undone in the software.** A person who withdrew cannot be set active again (409 on `/active`), cannot be assigned (already true: inactive) and cannot be edited (409 on `/edit`). A person who agrees again gets a new profile with a new consent and a new alias. This keeps the withdrawal date meaningful and stops a quick click from reversing a decision the person made.
- **E. The export leaves out their readings.** `GET /api/firefighters/readings.csv` omits every reading whose firefighter has withdrawn, before and after the withdrawal, not only the later ones. A reading nobody wore stays, with an empty firefighter field. The default was chosen by the developer as the safer one and is easy to loosen later: dropping one condition in services/firefighter_export.py would keep them under the alias. The data-governance owner decides whether that default stays.
- **F. The list.** `GET /api/firefighters` rows get `consent_withdrawn_ts` (null when not withdrawn), the 16th key.
- **G. Dashboard.** Each row in Settings has Edit and Withdraw consent. Edit opens a form with the same fields and ranges as the Add form, starting at the host's values; only the fields that differ are sent, and a form with no change sends nothing ('Nothing was changed.'). Withdraw consent asks first ('This cannot be undone here.'), then calls the route. A withdrawn person shows 'Consent withdrawn' in the Status column and has no buttons. The readings note says people who withdrew are left out. An older host that sends no `consent_withdrawn_ts` is treated as not withdrawn.

## Not here (open points)

- **The readings stay in the database.** Withdrawing keeps the stored frames and the assignment history; only the per-firefighter export leaves them out. The frozen full CSV (design 2.5) carries a node, not a person, so it is not changed; the existing backups and USB copies are not touched. Whether withdrawal also requires deleting the person's readings, their profile or their name is a decision for the governance owner and the RA 10173 contact. The software has no erase route; `purge-session` (ADR 0039) works on a session, not a person, and has its known limits.
- **No history of old values.** An edit overwrites. If the forecasting work needs the weight a person had on a given day, a profile history table is a later part.
- **The withdrawal date is the time of the click.** A withdrawal made on paper earlier is not back-dated.
- **No admin role.** Any logged-in operator may edit a profile or withdraw consent, like every other route (ADR 0071). Withdrawal is the action where an admin-only role matters most.
- **End shift still keeps the assignments** (ADR 0072).
- **Rev 3.46 / the Word documents** do not describe edit and withdraw yet; they are covered by the consolidated revision.
